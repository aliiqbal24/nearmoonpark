# Moonpark implementation

Moonpark is a single-player lunar theme park. The player owns rides and land; visitors and concessionaires are NPCs. The 40-second skippable, narrated opening film introduces the first tourists and an optimistic Moon-colonization dream. A ten-second shuttle approach holds the visible clock at 9:00; the playable opening day then lasts 540 simulation seconds (550 total ticks) at 1×, 2× or 4× speed. The park remains visible and explorable at closing.

## The loop

Twelve inbound shuttles bring eight visitors each. Shuttles visit the spaceport every forty simulation seconds, descend, dock, unload and collect homebound guests. Later flights continue collecting departures. Guests visibly walk the gangway; they wait for an actual boarding window before the contract marks them gone. Visitors have finite wallets, different thrill preferences, hunger and thirst. They walk along connected paths, choose affordable destinations, queue, pay, ride and form opinions. Repeating the same ride gives diminishing satisfaction. Hovering or selecting a guest reveals their actual state.

The player starts with 1,600 credits, a Lunar Bounce and a food concession. Available construction is deliberately small: Bounce (300), Earthrise Wheel (600), Rover Rally (800), Mass-Driver Coaster (1,200), food/drink kiosks (150), maintenance depot (250) and solar canopy (300). Money is stored as integer centicredits. Paths cost two credits per new tile. Smart connection uses a 0–1 breadth-first search to minimize newly purchased tiles; existing paths are free to reuse. Paths can be erased, and walkers reroute. The gate cannot be erased.

Coasters have two footprint blueprints, rotation and three launch settings. Gentle/standard/extreme cycles take 36/30/24 seconds, consume 2/3/4 power and incur 2/4/6 wear per cycle. Capacity upgrades add half the base seats and one power demand. Solar canopies add four power to the base eight. Condition affects operation and resale. Selling a ride returns 70% of invested value multiplied by its remaining condition, so buying, wearing out and reselling rides cannot mint credits.

Concessionaires have their own finite cash and inventories. They set prices through deterministic policies responding to recent demand and scarcity. The player chooses placement and revenue share, or invests 100 credits. Supplies are purchased upfront and arrive after fifteen seconds; connected delivery rovers represent those actual orders. Operating costs are charged every fifteen seconds. A tenant unable to pay closes until investment restores operating cash. The depot buys parts and charges for repairs. No cosmetic delivery changes the economic record.

Every transfer has a sender, recipient, amount and reason. Goods have explicit stock, deliveries and consumption. The ledger shows a bounded recent history; the simulation retains cumulative totals for scoring and conservation tests. Lunar credits are virtual currency, not redeemable tokens.

## Merchant partnerships

Omar, Mina and Nia offer one optional partnership each. Only one can be active at a time. The player transfers 100 credits of working capital to the merchant; the Tourism Board reserves the advertised grant from its finite 600-credit opening budget. Omar sells eight meals in 160 seconds for a 180-credit grant. Mina sells eight drinks in 140 seconds for 200. Nia completes one repair in 170 seconds for 220. Later offers open as the day develops; the existing park-building hints take precedence during the first few moments.

Progress counts work performed after acceptance. Completion releases the grant once; a missed deadline returns unclaimed funding to the Board, while the merchant keeps its invested capital. These transfers conserve the same virtual credit supply as every other transaction. NEAR validates the signed command, reserves funding and emits a `partnership_paid` event upon settlement. `get_partnerships` exposes the canonical terms and reserves. The browser persists agreement/payment transaction links across reloads. The grant is virtual currency. Separately, acceptance issues the player a NEP-171 concession: a fixed 20% share of that shop’s remaining opening-day sales. Rent changes and demolition are rejected after signing. The share keeps earning even if the service-target grant expires. `earned` counts actual post-agreement revenue-share receipts; goods, cash and stock conservation remain unchanged.

The concession certificate carries the right to a capped funded testnet reward, paid at 0.0001 tNEAR per earned lunar credit, up to its reserved backing (at most 0.02 tNEAR). The contract reserves that backing from a separate finite deposit pool at issuance; unused backing returns at closing. Current ownership is read from the standard NFT owner map. Transfer moves the uncollected claim; the park operation, game credits and score stay with the founder. Failed payment callbacks restore claimability; pending/paid amounts prevent double spending. NFT transfer-call callbacks lock reward claims until ownership acceptance resolves. Standard core, approval, metadata and enumeration use near-contract-standards. Testnet rewards have no cash value.

Income feedback comes from actual ledger receipts: grouped ticket sales, revenue shares, refunds and partnership rewards. Coin trails point toward the cash HUD, the balance counts smoothly and positive receipts trigger sound. At high simulation speed, nearby receipts merge rather than filling the screen with repeated notifications. Merchant owners stand beside their businesses and remain selectable.

## Scoring and competition

The contract calculates a maximum 10,000 points: 6,000 for satisfaction, 2,500 for positive equity gain (capped at 1,500 credits) and 1,500 for distinct visitor/ride-family experiences (capped at three per visitor). Guests need a completed ride to contribute satisfaction. Bronze is 3,500, Silver 5,500 and Gold 7,000. Score details are available during play and at closing.

The public top five includes contract-verified guest exhibitions. Signed-in players have one opaque identity and one best result across their sponsored accounts. The hosted site uses the Sites social identity gateway; a random public pseudonym is the default. Guest and verification runs cannot enter the separate provisional prize standings. The cutoff is November 18, 2026 at 23:59 UTC; later results cannot displace that snapshot.

A separate 0.5 tNEAR escrow demonstrates funded competition payouts. After the cutoff, the sponsor may attest a Games-track or grand-prize award once. The contract itself chooses the first four frozen accounts and allocates 40/30/20/10, normalized if fewer qualify. Each account collects its own allocation; failed transfers remain retryable, and successful or pending claims cannot be duplicated. There is no administrator-selected payout recipient or general withdrawal. Human attestation is still required for the external hackathon result; the chain does not know that result by itself.

The personal USD pledge remains separate: US$400/300/200/100 after a Games track first-place award, or US$2,000/1,500/1,000/500 after the grand prize. No qualifying win means no USD payout. This pledge is not funded by testnet tokens; eligibility review and mainnet payments follow the published terms. The fifth place stays visible without a scheduled cash award.

## NEAR architecture

`crates/moon-sim` is the shared deterministic Rust engine. It compiles to native code for tests and replays, browser WebAssembly for responsive prediction, and a dependency of `crates/moon-contract`. The contract build excludes the browser's raw WASM ABI. Build the browser and contract separately to avoid Cargo feature unification.

The client submits commands rather than claimed balances, states or scores. `create_run` admits a sponsored account; `apply_actions` validates ownership, revision, idempotent request ID, commands, costs and elapsed time. Batches have at most sixteen commands and twenty simulation ticks. The contract limits simulation advancement to four times real elapsed time plus a small initial headroom, and rejects new decisions after a 565-second maximum wall deadline: ten approach seconds, nine playable minutes and fifteen seconds of bounded confirmation/handoff grace. The grace adds no simulation ticks, spending opportunities or score. Passive settlement can finish an interrupted round after its deadline.

The browser predicts up to forty ticks ahead, persists its operation journal and rebases on canonical contract responses. Only one transaction is submitted at a time. It uses finalized `EXECUTED` results, avoiding a wait for unrelated gas-refund receipts. A timed-out submission is read back and retried idempotently. Unconfirmed decisions arriving after closing are excluded and labelled. A result stays pending until canonical confirmation.

The sponsor creates testnet subaccounts with a browser-generated recovery key and a restricted session key. The server receives public keys only. The session key can call `create_run`, `apply_actions`, `claim_business_reward` and `claim_prize`, has a finite allowance and cannot attach deposits. Initial funding is 0.5 testnet NEAR, with a 0.4 NEAR session allowance. Sponsorship has per-IP and global limits. The browser checks the remaining account balance and key allowance before a new round. Private keys are stored locally; public exports exclude them.

The v3 contract is a keyless child account, separate from the sponsor. Onchain access-key queries return an empty list and the code exposes no upgrade method. Separate business/prize liability counters prevent new run storage from consuming reserved backing. The sponsorship server checks storage headroom before admitting a new account. `fund_storage` retains only needed new sponsorship and refunds the remainder of that same deposit; it cannot withdraw old funds.

NPC businesses use contract-managed treasuries and deterministic policies, not independent wallet custody or language models. D1 stores private authentication, profile and sponsorship records. The NEAR contract stores canonical game outcomes and rankings. This implementation does not claim NEAR Intents or NEP-245 support.

## Presentation boundary

The persistent Three.js park, animated visitors, occupied rides, delivery rovers, recurring shuttles, coaster camera and rendered opening film are functional. The film includes an original score, narration and sound design, with a live fallback and reduced-motion presentation. Further character/ride artwork and the submission demo follow gameplay review.
